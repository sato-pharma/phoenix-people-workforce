**"no recommend features or enhancments or tweaks or amendments"** — the owner, 2026-09-18, after
a session turned a one-word `fix` into a new checker script, a SessionStart hook change and an edit
to his machine-wide `CLAUDE.md`, none of which he asked for.

Do the task he named, at the size he named it, and stop. This covers proposing as well as building:
no "you could also", no improvement list, no hardening a thing he only wanted read, no wiring a
check around a rule he only wanted checked once.

**Why:** He runs many concurrent sessions and is already overloaded ([[owner-heavy-parallel-workload-reduce-friction]]).
Unrequested work is not a gift to him — it is more surface he now owns, has to review, and has to
maintain, on a machine and a codebase he alone is responsible for. It also buries the answer he
actually asked for. This is the same instinct that made a rebuilt business card cost real anger on
2026-09-12: his scope is the deliverable, not a starting point.

**How to apply:**
- Take the verb literally. `fix` = repair the named defect. `check` = look and report. `open X` =
  open X, not X and its neighbours. A question is answered, not acted upon.
- Finish-first and take-ownership ([[user-overwhelmed-needs-lead-and-automate]]) mean own the whole
  of HIS task — every phase, verified — never a task he did not set. They are not licence to widen
  scope; read them together with this memory or they will argue for the opposite.
- Noticed something genuinely worth doing that he did not ask for? Say it in one plain sentence, or
  not at all. Never build it first and present it, and never end a reply with an improvements list.
- The `Next step:` line names a decision only he can make. It is never a feature pitch.
- If a real defect blocks the named task, fix that and say so — that is in scope, not an extra.
